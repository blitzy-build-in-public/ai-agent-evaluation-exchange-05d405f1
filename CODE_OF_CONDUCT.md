# Code of conduct

ADEM — the Agent Development Exchange and Marketplace — is a public, MIT-licensed foundation whose
first version runs locally with no account, no external service and no secret. This document states
the conduct expected of everyone who takes part in it: in an issue, a pull request, a discussion, a
code review, a standards proposal, a commit message, a document or any other place where someone
speaks for the project. It is adapted from the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1, and it applies
equally to a developer, a buyer, a community contributor and an operator.

`CONTRIBUTING.md` is the contribution path — what a checkout needs, which command proves which kind of
change, which paths a maintainer reviews, and the checklist every pull request carries. `SECURITY.md`
is how a suspected vulnerability is reported. This document is the conduct those two assume.

## 1. Our pledge

We as members, contributors and leaders pledge to make participation in this community a
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and
healthy community.

## 2. Our standards

**Behaviour that contributes to a positive environment for this community includes:**

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Accepting responsibility and apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the
  experience.
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community.

**Behaviour that is unacceptable includes:**

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or electronic address, without their
  explicit permission.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

**Two of these bear on this project in a particular way, because of what it is.**

This is a foundation exchange for agent developers, and the discussion it invites — about a listing's
claims, an evaluation's evidence, a badge's scope, a standard's case set or pass threshold, or a
simulated transaction — is a discussion about evidence and behaviour. It is never about a person's
competence, intelligence or identity. Disagreeing with a measurement, a threshold, a case set, a
design decision or a review outcome is exactly the kind of contribution this project wants and is
welcome however firmly it is argued; attacking the person who made the thing is not.

Publishing another person's private information is prohibited in every form, and it covers anything
that would identify a real person: a name, a contact address, a telephone number, a financial detail,
a credential or token, or an excerpt of private or nonpublic material. The demo accounts, the seed
data and the fixtures are synthetic and carry this project's own fictional handles and organisations,
and none of them may be replaced with a real identity, in a contribution or in a comment about one.
The same rule governs a conduct report: describe what happened and who was involved as the project
already names them, and paste no new personal information into a report.

## 3. Enforcement responsibilities

Community leaders — the maintainers of this repository — are responsible for clarifying and enforcing
these standards of acceptable behaviour, and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in
response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and the responsibility to remove, edit or reject comments, commits,
code, issues, discussions, documents, proposals and other contributions that are not aligned with
this code of conduct, and will communicate the reasons for a moderation decision when it is
appropriate to do so. A moderation decision concerns conduct and never a technical position: a
contribution that argues for a different threshold, a different case set or a different design is
answered with evidence and reasoning, however firmly it is put.

## 4. Scope

This code of conduct applies within all of this project's community spaces — issues, pull requests,
discussions, code review, the standards proposal process, and every document, comment, example and
commit message the repository carries — and it also applies when an individual is officially
representing the project in a public space. Representing the project includes using a project
address, posting through a project account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or
offline event.

It applies to every role this project has. A **developer** publishes listings and versions, runs
evaluations and answers challenges. A **buyer** searches, filters and compares listings, reads
limitations, asks challenges and inquiries, and runs a simulated checkout. A **community
contributor** proposes standards, discusses designs and contributes code, documentation and fixture
packs. An **operator** moderates listings, reviews proposals, rules on appeals and reads the
append-only record. A reader who takes part in none of those is still held to this document, and each
of those roles is held to it toward the others.

The product's own vocabulary narrows nothing here. A report is labelled `SAMPLE` or
`VERIFIED-IN-DEMO`, a badge's scope is `structured output on fixture set only`, a simulated amount is
an integer count of minor units of the synthetic currency `DEMO`, and ADEM Shield Basic answers
`allow`, `deny` or `needs_approval`. None of those is a claim of certification, of safety or of a
guaranteed pass — buying an evaluation never guarantees one, because outcomes depend on test evidence
only — and none of them is a statement about a person.

## 5. Enforcement guidelines

Community leaders will follow these guidelines in determining the consequences of any action they
deem in violation of this code of conduct.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact.** The use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed unprofessional or
unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence.** A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the
nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology
may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact.** A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the people
involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the code of conduct, for a specified
period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels
such as social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or a permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Community impact.** A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate
behaviour.

**Consequence.** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the
community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved,
including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the code of conduct, is allowed during this
period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact.** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement
of classes of individuals.

**Consequence.** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## 6. Reporting a concern

**Report it privately, through the hosting platform's private channel to the maintainers of this
repository** — the repository's own page on the platform offers a private reporting path to its
maintainers, and a private message to them is an equivalent route — and not in a public issue, a pull
request, a discussion or a post. A report about conduct and a report about a vulnerability travel the
same way; `SECURITY.md` states the vulnerability half of it.

This repository publishes no email address, no telephone number and no personal contact detail
anywhere, and this document carries none either. That is deliberate rather than an oversight:

- The repository is public and MIT-licensed, and a real contact detail is itself a prohibited item in
  it. Nothing here may carry real personal information, a contact detail, a credential or a secret,
  and a conduct report must not introduce one.
- A private channel keeps the report between the reporter and the maintainers until a decision is
  reached, which is the point of reporting privately. A public post would disclose the concern to
  everyone at once and would often disclose the person it concerns at the same time.
- The platform channel keeps the report, the discussion and the decision in one place, so the outcome
  and the reasoning behind it stay together and can be revisited.

If you cannot reach a private channel, ask a maintainer for one in the repository rather than setting
the concern out in public: an issue that says only that you would like a private word with a
maintainer is enough for one to be arranged. A suspected vulnerability is never reported in the
tracker at all, and the private route for it is the one `SECURITY.md` describes.

**What happens to a report.** All complaints are reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly, by
maintainers other than anyone a report names, so far as the maintainer group allows. All community
leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and the security of the reporter of any incident: what a
report contains is not repeated publicly, the reporter is not identified without their agreement, and
the decision and its reasoning are communicated to the reporter. Where the report concerns a
contribution, the maintainers may remove, edit or reject it, and will say why when it is appropriate
to do so.

**What this does not reach.** A concern about conduct in a space this project does not control — a
downstream adopter's own forum or issue tracker, for instance — is not something these maintainers
can act in, and they will say so plainly rather than appearing to have taken it up. This document
also offers no response-time commitment, no service level and no appeal against a decision beyond
raising it once with the maintainers; `SECURITY.md` states the same absence of a service level for a
vulnerability.

## 8. This code applies to every contributor, in every project space

This code of conduct applies to every person who takes part in this project — a developer, a buyer, a
community contributor, an operator, a maintainer, and anyone reading or discussing it — and in every
space the project has: an issue, a pull request, a discussion, a code review, a standards proposal, a
review comment, a commit message, a document, an example, and any other place where someone
represents the project.

Being outside the roles this product defines changes nothing: a reader who posts in a discussion is
held to this document exactly as a maintainer is. Where a role carries power over someone else's
contribution or record — an operator ruling on an appeal, a maintainer reviewing a protected path, a
reviewer deciding whether a change is merged — that power is exercised under this document, never as
a licence to treat a person badly.

The contribution path is `CONTRIBUTING.md`, the private route for a suspected vulnerability is
`SECURITY.md`, and the process by which a standard's criteria change is
`docs/governance/SGI-PROPOSALS.md`. Each assumes this document, and this document restates none of
them.
